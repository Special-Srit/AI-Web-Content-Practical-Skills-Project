# Spotify

## UI and interaction

**Mobile**

- Bottom navigation centers on Home, Search, and Your Library.
- A compact Now Playing bar sits above the navigation bar. Tapping it opens the full player.
- The full player exposes play/pause, skip, shuffle, repeat, save, device connection, and queue access.
- Queue behavior is explicit: swipe right to add, long-press and drag to reorder, swipe left to remove.
- Your Library supports filters, search, sorting, pinning, and grid/list views. [Primary: Spotify queue documentation](https://support.spotify.com/gm/article/play-queue/), [Primary: Now Playing documentation](https://support.spotify.com/me-en/article/now-playing/), [Primary: Library documentation](https://support.spotify.com/kr-ko/article/your-library/), [Primary: sorting/filtering documentation](https://support.spotify.com/st-en/article/sort-and-filter/)

**Desktop/web**

- Library becomes a left sidebar rather than a mobile menu bar.
- Playback remains in a persistent bottom bar.
- Queue opens beside the bottom player.
- Desktop supports custom playlist ordering; mobile mainly uses filters and sorting. [Primary: Spotify Library and desktop queue documentation](https://support.spotify.com/kr-ko/article/your-library/)

## Business model

Spotify Free is ad-supported. In Korea, Spotify’s own support page says that after Premium cancellation, the account returns to Free with advertising and shuffle playback. [Primary](https://support.spotify.com/kr-ko/article/cancel-premium/)

Current Korean prices, verified on Spotify’s Korean pricing page:

- Premium Basic: ₩8,690/month; no music downloads
- Premium Individual: ₩11,990/month
- Premium Duo: ₩17,985/month
- Premium Student: ₩6,600/month
- Individual, Duo, and Student include offline downloads; Basic does not.
- The Student plan requires an approved higher-education institution and age 18+, so a vocational high-school student should not assume eligibility. [Primary: Spotify Korea pricing](https://www.spotify.com/kr-ko/premium/?nd=1)

Spotify’s product is built around licensed music plus podcasts, audiobooks, and increasingly video. Spotify’s license terms mean downloaded content remains inside Spotify rather than becoming user-owned files. [Primary](https://support.spotify.com/kr-ko/article/what-is-spotify/)

## Strengths

Spotify’s strongest structural advantage is discovery:

- Personalized playlists such as Discover Weekly, Release Radar, Mixes, and Daylist are generated from listening behavior and similarity data.
- Spotify has an enormous cross-market user base, creating more behavioral data for recommendation systems.
- Spotify Connect lets a phone control playback on another logged-in device, speaker, TV, or car. [Primary: personalized playlists](https://support.spotify.com/pw/article/find-playlists/), [Primary: Spotify Connect](https://support.spotify.com/ws/article/spotify-connect/)

This makes Spotify better than a simple local player when the user wants “surprise me with something suitable.”

## Weaknesses and complaints

- The Free mobile experience deliberately makes precise playback less convenient through ads and shuffle restrictions. This is a verified product restriction, not merely a complaint. [Primary](https://support.spotify.com/kr-ko/article/cancel-premium/)
- App-store reviews repeatedly mention confusing queues, unwanted recommendation tracks, playback bugs, and background playback stopping. These are user reports, not controlled usability research. [Secondary: Google Play reviews](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.spotify.music), [Secondary: App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/spotify-music-and-podcasts/id324684580?platform=undefined&see-all=reviews)
- Spotify’s growing mixture of music, podcasts, audiobooks, video, sponsored recommendations, and AI features can make the core “play this song” task feel less direct. This is an assessment based on the verified product expansion, not a company-confirmed defect. [Primary](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-02-10/spotify-q4-2025-earnings/)

## Distinctive features

Within this comparison set:

- Spotify Connect device handoff
- Jam, where multiple people add and reorder songs together
- Discover Weekly and Daylist
- AI DJ, which combines personalized music with spoken commentary
- Mature queue editing and library filtering

[Primary: Jam](https://support.spotify.com/st-en/article/jam/), [Primary: AI DJ](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-05-07/dj-expansion-4-new-languages/)

# YouTube Music

## UI and interaction

**Mobile**

- Home shows personalized stations and recommendations based on YouTube and YouTube Music listening history.
- Explore contains popular releases, genres, playlists, podcasts, and episodes.
- Library contains saved songs, playlists, albums, podcasts, and downloads.

## Business model

The free, ad-supported tier currently includes:

For music, Premium adds:

- Ad-free music

[Primary: YouTube Music free/Premium comparison](https://support.google.com/youtubemusic/answer/6313529?hl=en)

In Korea, YouTube Premium costs ₩14,900/month according to an official KT product page quoting YouTube’s regular price. ## Strengths

YouTube Music is structurally better at finding unusual versions:

- The YouTube video catalog contains performances and remixes that may not exist in a conventional licensed-audio catalog.
- Recommendations can use listening behavior from the wider YouTube ecosystem.

- Samples provides vertically swipeable short previews of unfamiliar music. [Primary: Samples](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/taking-music-discovery-to-the-next-level-with-samples/), [Primary: Sound Search](https://support.google.com/youtubemusic/answer/13401025?hl=en)

# UI/UX Lessons for the 7-Day Project

## Worth Borrowing

### 1. A persistent mini-player with one obvious expansion path

Borrow Spotify and Apple’s structure:

- Fixed bottom player, approximately 56–72px tall
- Artwork thumbnail
- Song title and artist
- Play/pause button
- Queue button
- Tap the bar to expand into the full player
- Full player contains scrubber, previous/next, repeat, and file details

Do not create several competing player states. One mini-player and one full-player screen are enough.

### 2. A deliberately simple queue

Use a plain JavaScript array:

```text
queue = [
  { id, title, artist, fileName }
]
```

Support only:

- Play now
- Play next
- Add to end
- Remove
- Clear queue
- Optional drag handle for reordering

Use visible buttons in addition to gestures. Spotify’s swipe-to-remove pattern is convenient, but a small student project should not make essential actions invisible.

### 3. A touch-friendly scrubber

Use one horizontal range control and map its value to:

```js
audio.currentTime = audio.duration * value;
```

Show elapsed time and remaining/total time. A 44px-high touch target is more important than animated waveform graphics.

The browser’s `<audio>` element already provides playback events, seeking, buffering, and duration information. [Primary: MDN audio element](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/audio), [Primary: MDN media seeking](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Audio_and_video_delivery)

### 4. A useful empty state

When no files exist, show:

- “Add audio files”
- Supported formats
- “Your files stay on this device”
- A short explanation that refreshing may require selecting files again

After files exist, show:

- Recently added
- Recently played
- Favorites
- Playlists

Do not fill an empty state with fake recommendations. A local player has no recommendation engine yet; pretending otherwise weakens trust.

### 5. Small-library navigation

For a seven-day app, use three main destinations:

- Home
- My Library
- Player

Put Search inside Library unless the file count becomes large. Search should filter local metadata immediately by title, artist, album, and filename.

Borrow Spotify’s filters and pinned items, but implement only:

- All files
- Favorites
- Playlists
- Recently added

### 6. Local file handling that respects the constraint

The browser can receive user-selected audio through `<input type="file" accept="audio/*">` and create temporary playback URLs with `URL.createObjectURL(file)`. [Primary: MDN File API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/File_API/Using_files_from_web_applications)

The important limitation is storage:

- `localStorage` stores string data and has browser-dependent limits.
- It should store metadata, playlist IDs, favorites, and settings—not entire audio files.
- Object URLs are temporary references to selected files.
- After a refresh, the browser may require the user to select the files again.

MDN explicitly warns that client-side storage limits vary by browser. IndexedDB is the more appropriate technology for storing large binary files, but that would expand the project beyond the stated localStorage-only scope. [Primary: MDN client-side storage](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development/Extensions/Client-side_APIs/Client-side_storage)

### 7. Explicit playback state

Make the UI show:

- Which file is playing
- Whether playback is paused
- Whether the queue is empty
- Whether the selected file is missing
- Whether the browser rejected playback

This is better than silently failing. Local-file playback has no server fallback.

## Traps to Avoid

### 1. Building a recommendation engine

Spotify’s recommendations depend on:

- Listening history
- Large catalog metadata
- Similar-user behavior
- Ranking models
- Continuous server-side experimentation

You can implement deterministic “Recently played” or “More from this artist” locally. Do not promise AI recommendations in seven days.

### 2. Copying Samples, AI DJ, Music Tuner, or Sound Search

These features require large catalogs, machine-learning services, speech/audio recognition, or a major content platform. They are excellent research references but poor seven-day implementation targets.

### 3. Copying Jam, Connect, or account synchronization

Real-time shared queues and cross-device playback require:

- Login
- Backend state
- WebSocket or polling coordination
- Conflict handling
- Device identity
- Permission rules

Your project has no backend, login, or database. Treat the queue as one-device state.

### 4. Promising native-style background playback

A browser `<audio>` element can play local audio, but mobile browser behavior, lock-screen controls, autoplay restrictions, and background execution are platform-dependent. Test iPhone Safari early and describe the result honestly. Do not promise Spotify-level background behavior without verification.

### 5. Storing audio blobs in localStorage

This is the most important technical trap. Base64-encoding songs into localStorage will quickly hit quota and create slow, fragile state. Store metadata only, then ask the user to reselect files after refresh.

### 6. Implementing a huge catalog interface

A catalog of millions of tracks justifies:

- Search ranking
- Artist pages
- Genre browsing
- Editorial shelves
- Recommendations
- Caching
- Pagination
- Missing-track handling
- Rights and region logic

A local-player project should have a small, transparent library. Do not reproduce a streaming service’s information architecture when the app has only the user’s files.

### 7. Overusing gestures

Swipes are attractive in demos but risky for accessibility and discoverability. Use gestures only for optional shortcuts:

- Swipe down to close full player
- Long-press for a context menu
- Drag handle to reorder

Keep buttons for play, pause, next, delete, favorite, and queue actions.

## Mobile Blind Spots to Exploit

### 1. No surprise autoplay

YouTube Music users frequently complain that autoplay or queue behavior adds unexpected recommendations or versions. A focused local tool can make the rule explicit:

- “Queue ends here”
- “Autoplay off”
- “Add next” means exactly next
- “Add to queue” means exactly the bottom

This is a defensible product difference because it removes uncertainty rather than competing on catalog size.

### 2. Clear file identity

Streaming services can show multiple versions of the same song. Local files can be clearer:

```text
Title
Artist
filename.mp3
03:42
```

Show the source filename in a secondary line. This is especially useful when students have several live, remix, instrumental, and exported versions.

### 3. Honest offline behavior

Melon, Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube Music use licensed downloads with DRM and subscription checks. A local-file player can make a simpler promise:

- These are files the user supplied.
- No account is needed.
- No server upload occurs.
- The app plays only files currently available to the browser.

### 4. Less crowded home screen

Large services combine music, podcasts, audiobooks, videos, charts, ads, artist news, and recommendations. A student project can deliberately have:

- One clear “Add files” action
- One recently played section
- One playlist section
- One player

The blind spot is not missing features; it is the number of decisions users must make before listening.

### 5. Better queue visibility

Make the queue a first-class screen or bottom sheet:

```text
Now playing
Next
Later
```

Show the active track separately from upcoming tracks. Include “Clear upcoming” without deleting the currently playing song. This avoids the ambiguity reported in Spotify, YouTube Music, Apple Music, and Amazon Music user feedback.

### 6. Better failure states

A focused tool can explain:

- “This file was moved or renamed.”
- “Please select it again.”
- “This browser cannot decode this format.”
- “The queue item was removed because the file is unavailable.”

That is a meaningful improvement over a large service simply skipping to another song.

The strongest seven-day project position is therefore not “a smaller Spotify.” It is:
